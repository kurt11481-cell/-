# Semiconductor and AI-Infrastructure Analysis

## Map the economic layer

Classify the company before comparing it:

- accelerator or CPU design;
- custom ASIC and design services;
- foundry and process technology;
- HBM, DRAM, NAND, or enterprise storage;
- advanced packaging, substrate, or test;
- switch silicon, SerDes, DSP, retimer, or interconnect;
- optical laser, InP, transceiver, fiber, CPO, or NPO;
- server integration and rack-scale systems;
- power, cooling, grid, or data-center operations.

Compare companies within their economic layer first. A server assembler, optical component vendor, custom-silicon designer, and GPU platform should not receive the same margin or valuation assumptions.

## Track the commercialization stage

Never collapse these stages into one claim:

1. product announcement;
2. engineering sample;
3. customer qualification;
4. design win;
5. committed order or backlog;
6. volume production;
7. recognized revenue;
8. cash collection and sustainable gross profit.

State the latest verified stage, expected lag, cancellation risk, and evidence source. A sample or partnership is not revenue.

## Inspect subsector economics

### Custom ASIC and networking

Review NRE revenue, number and concentration of programs, tape-outs, production ramps, foundry dependency, SerDes generation, switch capacity, software ecosystem, customer insourcing, and the lag from design win to revenue.

### Memory and storage

Review bit shipments, ASP, cost per bit, inventory days, utilization, capex discipline, HBM mix, packaging capacity, customer qualification, contract versus spot pricing, and the risk that slowing price increases precede an earnings peak.

### Optical connectivity

Review 800G/1.6T/next-generation product mix, DSP and laser sourcing, InP capacity, yield, customer qualification, CPO/NPO adoption, pluggable coexistence, power per bit, and whether constrained components enable pricing or prevent shipment.

### Foundry and packaging

Review node mix, yield, wafer availability, advanced-packaging capacity, customer concentration, geographic concentration, depreciation intensity, and export controls.

### Servers and rack integration

Separate pass-through GPU revenue from gross profit. Review AI-server margin, backlog cancellation rights, customer financing quality, working-capital burden, vendor concentration, and service attach rates.

### Power, cooling, and data centers

Review book-to-bill, backlog conversion, lead times, power interconnection, rack density, liquid cooling, PUE, financing commitments, tenant credit, and whether debt-funded capex creates revenue or balance-sheet risk.

## Follow the funding chain

Distinguish companies that borrow to build capacity from suppliers that receive the resulting orders. Debt-funded AI capex can improve supplier visibility while increasing the borrower's refinancing and return-on-investment risk.

Check:

- free cash flow after capex;
- debt, lease, and purchase commitments;
- customer prepayments and warrants;
- circular investment or reciprocal purchase arrangements;
- credit spreads and refinancing calendar;
- evidence that end-user revenue supports infrastructure spending.

## Stress the thesis

Always test:

- hyperscaler capex deceleration;
- customer concentration and internal chip development;
- TSMC, HBM, packaging, laser, or power bottlenecks;
- export controls and China exposure;
- double ordering and inventory correction;
- price erosion and mix-driven margin compression;
- faster standards transitions;
- announced capacity arriving after the cycle peaks.

State whether a news item changes current earnings, future probability, valuation sentiment, or only the narrative.
